Why isn't electricity use in universities and schools under control?

In universities and schools, classrooms and labs keep different hours, holidays are frequent and equipment is varied. Lights and cooling in empty classrooms and equipment left on during closures create wasted consumption — while teaching, research and equipment safety come before cutting costs.

How is KAP's ESA installed, and how long does it take?

Installation starts with a short technical visit: the circuit map, measurement points and list of controllable loads are recorded. Then split-core current sensors (CT) are clamped onto the panel cables with no power cut and no rewiring, and the ESA is mounted on the wall next to the panel. Installation time depends on the number of panels and circuits and is confirmed for each site after the visit.

KAP in practice: from installation to results at your university or school

KAP works in five steps, and each step has a clear output that building managers, faculty and students can see.

  1. Scoping and site visit: decide which panels and circuits are monitored and which loads must never be controlled.
  2. Baseline: current consumption, past bills, working days and weather are recorded so a fair comparison is possible later.
  3. Understanding consumption: The ESA separates the consumption patterns of lighting and cooling in classrooms, halls and non-sensitive equipment, and shows wasted use.
  4. Alerts and safe control: an alert and a chance to fix it come first; if wasteful use continues, only pre-approved loads are controlled and every command is logged with its time and reason.
  5. Results report: energy saved, peak demand and contracted-capacity status are reported against the baseline so the next decision is based on real numbers.

Lower consumption lasts when users understand the reason for each recommendation and see the result; that's why KAP treats short training, simple reports and the ability to log exceptions as part of the service.

A real example: the University of Tehran pilot

KAP's pilot has been carried out at the University of Tehran's Faculty of World Studies: the project scope, measurement points, circuit map and list of controllable loads were recorded; alerts were then tuned to users' actual behaviour, and control of approved loads was switched on afterwards. The project management has reported a reduction in the demand index against the baseline. Development will continue from there to other University of Tehran buildings in Tehran.

What are the costs and terms?

Cost depends on the type of site. For homes and buildings eligible for an energy-saving certificate, KAP is deployed in return for an energy-saving certificate contract signed with the customer; control equipment such as smart plugs and controllers is supplied separately. The contract is based on verified savings, measured against the baseline following the IPMVP framework. For sites whose equipment KAP makes smart, such as facilities with solar panels or generators, the contract includes an installation and commissioning fee and a recurring monitoring fee. In both cases, the scope of work and contract details are set for each site after a technical visit. KAP does not promise the same savings to every site, because results depend on each site's conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Will laboratories and research equipment be switched off?

No. Sensitive lab loads, communications equipment and safety systems are on the exclusion list and are never controlled.

How are the academic calendar and holidays taken into account?

The baseline accounts for working days, holidays and changes of use, and control rules are set to each building's activity calendar.

Is it suitable for schools too?

Yes. Schools and smaller educational centres are covered with the same monitoring, alerts and approved control, using equipment sized to the building.

What does KAP offer students and staff?

Classroom comfort is maintained, and the money saved can go back into teaching and research. The KAP app can be used both by building managers and to teach good energy habits.

Does KAP also offer EV charging at universities?

KAP is pursuing cable EV-charging services at the pilot stage, and their development will follow grid-connection capacity and real demand.
