What is a virtual power plant (VPP), and how does it ease Iran's electricity shortfall?

📅 August 2026 ⏱ 8 min read ✍️ KAP R&D team

The growing gap between electricity supply and demand in Iran leaves the national grid more than 15,000–20,000 MW short in summer. The traditional fix is building new thermal power plants at a cost of billions of dollars and heavy environmental pollution — an approach that is slow and constrained by gas-fuel and capital limits. The world's newer answer is to form a virtual power plant (VPP) .

How exactly does a virtual power plant (VPP) work?

A virtual power plant is not made of giant steel turbines and sends no smoke into the sky. A VPP is essentially a centralised software system built on AI and the Internet of Things (IoT) that connects thousands of distributed energy resources (DERs):

  • Demand response: temporarily switching off or adjusting cooling systems, chillers and industrial pumps during critical grid peaks, with no noticeable loss of comfort.
  • Solar and wind renewables: integrating rooftop panels on buildings and distributed power plants.
  • Battery storage and backup diesel generators: activating batteries in the very seconds the grid frequency drops.
'Not consuming 1 kWh at grid peak is worth as much as — or more than — generating 1 kWh at the country's most expensive gas plant. This is called the negawatt.'

KAP's role in making VPP a reality in Iran

The KAP platform goes beyond a simple monitoring system or legacy BMS. KAP's architecture is designed to link consumers with the national grid:

  • Analysing and forecasting consumption patterns: KAP's machine-learning algorithms analyse each customer's behaviour (industrial, office, educational or residential) and calculate how much load each customer can shift at every hour of the day.
  • Fast response to dispatch needs: when frequency drops or the grid nears its generation ceiling, the KAP platform adjusts consumption at pre-agreed points — automatically or by agreement — without cutting whole lines.
  • The digital energy economy and tradable electricity value: customers who help shave the peak receive transparent financial rewards or economic credits.

Virtual power plants vs. forced blackouts

Today, traditional load management means cutting entire feeders to industrial parks or residential areas, causing heavy industrial losses and deep frustration. In KAP's virtual power plant:

no industry shuts down completely; non-critical processes are shifted, air conditioners are adjusted by 2 degrees and unnecessary lighting is dimmed. These smart decisions turn power cuts from a crippling threat into a shared economic opportunity.
