Help centre and FAQ

Answers to common questions about the KAP platform

Everything you need to know about the technology, pilot deployment, data security and economic savings.

Conventional BMS systems and traditional meters record only total consumption and need heavy wiring and expensive hardware. KAP goes beyond simple monitoring: it is an AI and IoT platform that uncovers hidden consumption patterns, forecasts load flexibility and makes the value of savings and demand management tradable through a smart energy-exchange layer (a virtual power plant).
No. One of KAP's founding principles is 'the user stays in control and operations continue'. In the pilot and operational phases, the system only offers precise analysis, opportunities to improve consumption and early warnings to managers or residents; no unwanted control is applied to production or residents' comfort. Automatic control can be enabled only for non-critical equipment, with the user's approval and their own settings.
No. KAP uses non-invasive clamp sensors (split-core CT) and lightweight IoT modules installed on the electrical panel in minutes, with no power cut and no changes to building cables. The platform can also connect directly to digital smart meters already on the grid.
As part of its social responsibility and to grow the energy-innovation ecosystem, KAP aims to deploy free pilots at universities and schools across the country. Selected homes and residential complexes can also sign up for the beta. For industry and large commercial complexes, a shared-savings partnership model is offered.
No cameras are installed at all, and privacy is protected. Using load-disaggregation (NILM) algorithms, the system learns an older person's daily routines from the kettle, fridge, lights and TV switching on. If there is a noticeable deviation (such as the kettle not coming on in the morning or the bathroom light staying on at midnight), a gentle alert is sent to the family.
A virtual power plant uses AI to pool the scattered load-reduction capacity of hundreds of buildings and factories. By voluntarily shifting load during grid peak hours, industries and complexes avoid sudden blackouts and receive financial rewards or savings-certificate credits.
Consumption data is transferred and stored on secure servers using advanced encryption (TLS/SSL and AES-256). Each customer's data is fully isolated, and no unauthorised access to process information, equipment names or trade secrets is possible.
