Demand-side management for the power sector: make load-reduction capacity visible

For the grid, the question isn't just 'how much electricity is used?'. What matters is knowing what capacity can be managed, where and when — and how the result of an action is verified.

Explore working together

Which power-sector companies is KAP right for?

KAP measures real consumption behind the meter, separates controllable loads and reports the result of every action against the baseline. This data is valuable to companies that generate, distribute, measure or supply electricity.

Tavanir and distribution companies

KAP shows what load, at what time and how much, can be shifted in each building and industry, so peak-load management can be done by selectively controlling equipment instead of area-wide blackouts.

  • Identifying and pooling customers' load-reduction capacity
  • Verifiable result reports for demand-response programmes
  • Helping relieve grid pressure at summer peaks

Solar plants and solar companies

Monitoring solar generation and building consumption together helps use the electricity generated on site more effectively and shift flexible loads to generating hours.

  • Matching consumption to panel generating hours
  • Reports on self-consumption and electricity exported to the grid
  • Data for designing and selling the right solar systems

Generator and emergency-power suppliers

During power cuts, KAP separates essential from non-essential loads so the generator supplies only what's needed, uses less fuel and is sized correctly.

  • Prioritising loads during blackouts
  • Helping choose the right generator capacity
  • Monitoring operation and consumption in emergency mode

Meter manufacturers and smart-reading systems

KAP's ESA does not replace the smart meter; with authorised access to data, it works on the existing infrastructure. The meter records total consumption, and KAP turns it into equipment-level analysis and action.

  • Adding an analytics and control layer to installed meters
  • Technical cooperation and data integration
  • Added value for meter makers' customers

Electrical equipment manufacturers

Makers of electrical panels, transformers, switches and protection equipment can build KAP monitoring into their products so customers see consumption and equipment health moment by moment.

  • Smart electrical panels with built-in CT sensors
  • Early warning of overloads and unusual behaviour
  • Performance data for after-sales service

Virtual power plants (VPP): the next dimension

When the scattered demand-management capacity of homes, buildings and industries is identified and pooled, together it can act as a 'virtual power plant' — without building a new power plant.

KAP provides a platform to identify, measure and verify this capacity, so its value can be calculated and used for grid stability.

How is KAP's ESA installed, and how long does it take?

Installation starts with a short technical visit: the circuit map, measurement points and list of controllable loads are recorded. Then split-core current sensors (CT) are clamped onto the panel cables with no power cut and no rewiring, and the ESA is mounted on the wall next to the panel. Installation time depends on the number of panels and circuits and is confirmed for each site after the visit.

What are the costs and terms?

Cost depends on the type of site. For homes and buildings eligible for an energy-saving certificate, KAP is deployed in return for an energy-saving certificate contract signed with the customer; control equipment such as smart plugs and controllers is supplied separately. The contract is based on verified savings, measured against the baseline following the IPMVP framework. For sites whose equipment KAP makes smart, such as facilities with solar panels or generators, the contract includes an installation and commissioning fee and a recurring monitoring fee. In both cases, the scope of work and contract details are set for each site after a technical visit. KAP does not promise the same savings to every site, because results depend on each site's conditions.

KAP in practice: from installation to results in the buildings and industries that work with you

KAP works in five steps, and each step has a clear output your company and its customers can see.

  1. Scoping and site visit: decide which panels and circuits are monitored and which loads must never be controlled.
  2. Baseline: current consumption, past bills, working days and weather are recorded so a fair comparison is possible later.
  3. Understanding consumption: The ESA separates customers' flexible-load consumption patterns at peak hours and shows wasted use.
  4. Alerts and safe control: an alert and a chance to fix it come first; if wasteful use continues, only pre-approved loads are controlled and every command is logged with its time and reason.
  5. Results report: energy saved, peak demand and contracted-capacity status are reported against the baseline so the next decision is based on real numbers.

Lower consumption lasts when users understand the reason for each recommendation and see the result; that's why KAP treats short training, simple reports and the ability to log exceptions as part of the service.

Common questions from power-sector companies

What is demand-side management?

Demand-side management means reducing or shifting consumption at peak hours instead of increasing generation. KAP does this by measuring each piece of equipment's consumption and selectively controlling approved loads, without cutting power to the whole building.

Does KAP cut off a customer's electricity?

No. KAP controls only loads the customer has approved in advance, and sensitive and essential loads are outside the control scope. Every action is logged and traceable.

How is KAP's ESA different from an existing smart meter?

A smart meter records total consumption for billing. KAP's ESA, with CT sensors on internal circuits, sees each piece of equipment's consumption, finds unnecessary use and measures the result of improvements against the baseline. The two complement each other.

How is the result of load reduction measured?

Using a baseline adjusted for working days, weather and changes of use, following the international IPMVP framework, so a drop caused by closures isn't wrongly credited to the system.
