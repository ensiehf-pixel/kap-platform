Why is electricity use high in office and commercial buildings?

In offices and commercial buildings, lights and cooling in empty rooms, equipment left on after hours and heavy loads running at the same time make up a large share of consumption. Exceeding the contracted capacity can also raise connection charges. The bill doesn't show these separately, and general reminders to staff don't produce lasting results.

How is KAP's ESA installed, and how long does it take?

Installation starts with a short technical visit: the circuit map, measurement points and list of controllable loads are recorded. Then split-core current sensors (CT) are clamped onto the panel cables with no power cut and no rewiring, and the ESA is mounted on the wall next to the panel. Installation time depends on the number of panels and circuits and is confirmed for each site after the visit.

KAP in practice: from installation to results in your office or commercial building

KAP works in five steps, and each step has a clear output that staff and the building manager can see.

  1. Scoping and site visit: decide which panels and circuits are monitored and which loads must never be controlled.
  2. Baseline: current consumption, past bills, working days and weather are recorded so a fair comparison is possible later.
  3. Understanding consumption: the ESA separates the consumption patterns of lighting, room cooling and heating and office equipment, and shows wasted use.
  4. Alerts and safe control: an alert and a chance to fix it come first; if wasteful use continues, only pre-approved loads are controlled and every command is logged with its time and reason.
  5. Results report: energy saved, peak demand and contracted-capacity status are reported against the baseline so the next decision is based on real numbers.

Lower consumption lasts when users understand the reason for each recommendation and see the result; that's why KAP treats short training, simple reports and the ability to log exceptions as part of the service.

What are the results? (a real example)

KAP matches consumption to actual working hours: lights and cooling in empty rooms are dimmed or switched off automatically, nobody's work stops and load stays within the contracted capacity. The real example is KAP's pilot at the University of Tehran's Faculty of World Studies, an office and teaching building where the project management has reported a reduction in the demand index against the baseline. Every report shows energy, peak demand and contracted capacity side by side.

What are the costs and terms?

Cost depends on the type of site. For homes and buildings eligible for an energy-saving certificate, KAP is deployed in return for an energy-saving certificate contract signed with the customer; control equipment such as smart plugs and controllers is supplied separately. The contract is based on verified savings, measured against the baseline following the IPMVP framework. For sites whose equipment KAP makes smart, such as facilities with solar panels or generators, the contract includes an installation and commissioning fee and a recurring monitoring fee. In both cases, the scope of work and contract details are set for each site after a technical visit. KAP does not promise the same savings to every site, because results depend on each site's conditions.

Frequently asked questions

How is KAP different from a building-management system (BMS)?

A BMS controls equipment on a fixed schedule. KAP measures each circuit's real consumption, finds unnecessary use, controls only approved loads and reports results against the baseline; it can also work alongside an existing BMS.

Will staff work be disrupted?

No. Essential loads, communications equipment and safety systems are on the exclusion list, and control happens only after an alert and only for approved loads. An authorised user can stop any command.

What is contracted capacity, and how does KAP help?

Contracted capacity is the power ceiling agreed in the electricity contract. By controlling when approved loads run at the same time, KAP helps keep the building's demand below that ceiling.

How are results measured?

Against an adjusted baseline, following the IPMVP framework, so that a drop caused by closures or a change of use isn't wrongly credited to the system.

Is it suitable for multi-storey buildings and complexes?

Yes. Each floor or panel is monitored separately, and reports are provided for the whole complex and for each section.
